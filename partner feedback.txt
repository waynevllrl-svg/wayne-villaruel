For this project, you and your partner will design, build, and present a personal portfolio website. You will use Git and GitHub to manage your project files and collaborate. Follow each step carefully and use the checklist to keep your work organized.

Part 1 – Define Your Portfolio Purpose Before writing any code, talk with your partner about your goals. Discuss these questions:

What is the main purpose of your portfolio?

Who are you making it for (your audience)?

What do you want visitors to think or feel about you?

Which skills or accomplishments do you want to show off?

Write a short purpose statement (2–3 sentences) for your portfolio. For example: “This portfolio introduces me as an aspiring web developer and highlights my skills and academic projects. It is mainly for potential employers and internship coordinators.”

Part 2 – Plan Your Website Structure Create a simple sitemap to show how your website will be organized. Your website must include these pages:

HOME

ABOUT ME

SKILLS

PROJECTS

CONTACT

You can add more pages if you want (such as Education, Certifications, Achievements, Resume, Services, or Experience).

Part 3 – Plan the Content for Each Page Decide what information will go on each page:

Home: Your name, a short introduction, and a professional tagline.

About Me: Your background, education, interests, and career goals.

Skills: List both technical and soft skills.

Projects: Describe at least 2 projects. For each, include the project title, a short description, technologies/tools used, your role, and an image or screenshot if you have one.

Contact: Professional contact information (do NOT include sensitive info like passwords or private addresses).

Part 4 – Build the HTML Pages Create the HTML files for your website in a folder named portfolio/.

Required files: index.html, about.html, skills.html, projects.html, contact.html.

Create a css/ folder for your stylesheet (style.css).

Create an images/ folder for your images.

Each page should use semantic HTML elements:

<header>

<nav>

<main>

Headings and sections

<footer>

Part 5 – Set Up Navigation Your navigation bar should appear the same on every page. It must include links to each main page:

HOME | ABOUT ME | SKILLS | PROJECTS | CONTACT

Make sure:

Every link works and points to the correct page.

Users can always return to the Home page easily.

Part 6 – Apply CSS Design Create and link a CSS stylesheet to style your website. Your design should show:

Typography: Use clear fonts, readable sizes, and clear headings.

Layout: Use proper spacing, organize sections, and keep alignment consistent.

Colors: Use color combinations that are easy to read and not overwhelming.

Visual Design: Keep styling consistent, create a clear visual order, use appropriate images/icons, and make your site look professional.

Part 7 – Make Your Site Accessible Review your portfolio for accessibility. Your site must include:

Proper heading order (hierarchy)

Good color contrast

Descriptive alt text for images

Readable text

Meaningful link text

Navigation that works with a keyboard

Clear labels for any forms

Ask your partner: “Can you understand and navigate my website without me explaining it?” If not, improve the areas that are confusing.

Part 8 – Test Your Website Before you present, check every page:

Content: All pages are present and complete. Check spelling and grammar. Make sure project descriptions are clear.

Navigation: All links work. Navigation is the same on every page. Users can get back to Home.

Design: Text is easy to read. Colors have enough contrast. Layout is tidy. There are no extra or unnecessary elements.

Technical: CSS is linked correctly. Images show up. No broken links. Pages look right on different screens.

Part 9 – Partner Evaluation Swap websites with another pair. Test their site and answer these:

Is navigation easy to use?

Is content organized?

Is text readable?

Is the design consistent?

Is the website accessible?

Do all links work?

Does the site look professional?

Give at least 3 specific suggestions for improvement. For example: “The Projects section is hard to read because the text and background colors are too similar.”

Part 10 – Refine Your Portfolio After getting feedback:

List the problems found.

Decide which issues are most important to fix.

Update your HTML and CSS.

Test your changes.

Review your website again to make sure it works well.

Remember: Develop → Test → Receive Feedback → Improve → Test Again.

Part 11 – Portfolio Progress Presentation Each pair will present their portfolio. In your presentation, explain:

The purpose of your portfolio.

Who your audience is.

Why you chose your website pages and navigation.

Why you chose your layout, colors, and fonts.

What accessibility features you included.

What problems you faced and how you solved them.

What improvements you made after testing and feedback.

What to Submit Each pair must turn in:

The working portfolio website (all HTML files, CSS, and images) in a GitHub repository.

A sitemap showing your website structure.

A content plan outlining what’s on each page.

A testing/feedback sheet from your partner pair.

A short presentation about your portfolio and the changes you made.

Throughout this project, use Git and GitHub to track your changes, share work with your partner, and submit your final website. Follow best practices for commits and collaboration.
